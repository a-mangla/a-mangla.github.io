Artificial Intelligence in Fighter Aircraft: A Critical Review of Decision Support, Human-AI Teaming, and Operational Limitations

I authored a critical review of how artificial intelligence could support pilots through cockpit decision support and human-AI collaboration, while examining the technological and human limitations of relying on these systems.

01 / ABSTRACT

Research abstract.

AI is increasingly being explored for integration into modern aircraft because of the growing need for time-pressured decisions in adversarial environments, as well as its potential to reduce pilot workload and speed up critical decision-making. This paper critically reviews current and proposed applications of AI in aviation, with a focus on human-AI teaming and decision support. A literature review was conducted using academic and technical sources covering applications of AI in military aviation, collaboration between humans and intelligent systems, as well as reliability and operational limitations. Papers focused on specific technologies were used to assess the system's current maturity level and the level of human involvement. The literature review suggests that AI offers the best benefit in bounded information tasks and rapid analysis of large datasets that would otherwise overwhelm the pilot, with an overall role in helping situational awareness. However, the paper also explores the limitations to these advantages. Incomplete training data and simulation-to-real discrepancies could surface as biases in the system’s outputs. Furthermore, systems remain vulnerable to adversarial interference. As AI implementation increases, so does pilots' dependence on these systems. If this relationship is not properly calibrated, blind trust could develop, eventually degrading pilots' skills as they no longer perform the tasks AI systems execute. The paper concludes that AI integration in fighter aircraft needs to prioritise reliable decision support and human-AI collaboration rather than fully replacing the judgement of the pilot.

02 / REVIEW FINDINGS

Where AI helps—and where caution matters.

The review considers the advantages of decision-support systems alongside reliability, operational and human-factors limitations. Three themes emerge from the literature discussed in the abstract.

01 / POTENTIAL

Supporting situational awareness.

AI may be most valuable for bounded information-processing tasks, including rapidly analysing data that would otherwise overwhelm a pilot.

02 / RELIABILITY

Recognising system limitations.

Incomplete training data, differences between simulations and real conditions, and adversarial interference can undermine reliable outputs.

03 / HUMAN FACTORS

Calibrating trust.

Overdependence on AI could encourage inappropriate trust and erode skills if pilots no longer practise tasks delegated to automated systems.

03 / CONCLUSION

Decision support, not a substitute for judgement.

The paper concludes that the priority should be trustworthy decision support and effective collaboration between human operators and AI—not the wholesale replacement of pilot judgement.
